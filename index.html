01 / Foundations

Two ways to
reframe design.

Participatory design expands who is involved in decision-making. Speculative design expands what can be imagined and discussed.

Working with people

Participatory
Design

People affected by a design should have a meaningful role in shaping it.

Participatory Design (PD) actively involves people throughout the design process—not merely as research subjects or testers, but as contributors to framing problems, generating ideas, making artefacts and assessing decisions.

Foundational reference: Schuler & Namioka (1993); Simonsen & Robertson (2013).

Working with possibilities

Speculative
Design

Design can make alternative futures tangible, contestable and open to debate.

Speculative Design uses artefacts, stories and scenarios to ask “what if?”. Rather than necessarily delivering a market-ready solution, it provokes questions about values, assumptions, consequences and possible futures.

Foundational reference: Dunne & Raby (2013).

02 / Critical positions

Solving problems.
Asking questions.

Dunne & Raby's A/B Manifesto contrasts affirmative and critical design. It is a provocation, rather than an absolute classification of all design practices.

03 / Futures

Which future?

The futures cone helps distinguish what is likely, what may happen, and what we might actively want.

01

Probable

What seems likely if current trends persist.

02

Plausible

What could credibly happen given what we know.

03

Possible

What can be imagined beyond current expectations.

04

Preferable

What people might collectively want to bring about.

05

Wild cards

Low-probability disruptions with major consequences.

06

Participation

Whose idea of a preferable future is being represented?

Futures cone: commonly associated with Joseph Voros; adapted and circulated through speculative design literature including Dunne & Raby.

06 / Hands-on activity

Design a
provotype.

Use a speculative prototype to challenge assumptions about AI and UX in the near future—not to solve the brief, but to make its consequences tangible and debatable.

Working in groups, combine prompts from three sets of cards. Build a provotype: a provocative concept, object, interface or service that exposes a tension in how AI might shape UX practice. The prompts are starting constraints, not a specification for a finished product.

01 / DEAL THE CARDS

Set the conditions.

Each group receives a value tension and deployment context from Deck A, an actor (or actors) and a rule the system must follow from Deck B, and a wildcard from Deck C. Use these to define what is at stake, for whom, and what might unexpectedly change.

A4—A · Value tensions + contexts ↗A4—B · Actors + rules ↗A4—C · Wildcards ↗
02 / MATERIALISE THE IDEA

Make it questionable.

Develop a speculative AI/UX provotype that makes the tension visible. Map the context, actors, rules and wildcard onto the A3 board. Consider what the system does, who benefits, who bears the cost, and what it makes possible—or impossible.

A3—1 · Provotype board ↗
03 / WRITE THE SCENE

Give it to another group.

Use the A5 role-play sheet to write a short enactment script. Describe the context, the technology, the actors, what they are trying to do, their purpose, and what happens next. Hand the script to another group without explaining the design first.

A5—1 · Role-play instructions ↗
04 / ENACT AND REFLECT

See what emerges.

The receiving group performs the scene. Discuss what they inferred from the provotype: whose values dominated, what was uncomfortable or surprising, and how different people might experience that AI-mediated future.

The design challenge

What should we question about the way AI could reshape UX—not just what could we build with it?

The printable worksheets are supplied in Portuguese, as in the workshop materials. The activity instructions here are in English.
